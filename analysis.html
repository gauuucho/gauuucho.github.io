01  Verdict

He sees it. He just does not always act on what he sees.

A competent Premium Liiga starter at 21, individually strong in contact and reliable with the ball at his feet. The gap to the next level is not athletic and it is not execution. It sits in the second stage of a football action, deciding what to do with the information he has already collected.

Scanning is visible in 46 of his 55 logged actions, which is a high rate and consistent across both halves. He gathers the picture. What he does with it is the problem. On four occasions he scans, sees the space behind his line, and does not adjust his depth before the pass is released. Two of those became goals. Stepping out of the line is a different failure at a different stage: the decision to step is usually right and the arrival is wrong, because he commits to the tackle rather than delaying and there is nobody behind him when it misses.

Duelling is where the report turns positive and it is the reason he is worth watching again. He wins contests of every type, aerial, frontal and side to side, and he wins them after an initial action has already failed.

Strengths

  • Defensive duelling of every type. Nine of ten duels won, including duels recovered after a failed interception.
  • Anticipation of the long ball. He starts the recovery run before the pass is released rather than reacting to it.
  • Build-up against a low block. Offers the angle, receives on the half turn, first touch into space, scans before releasing. No unsuccessful pass in this phase across 90 minutes.
  • Progressive passing range when given time, including passes between the lines and switches into the final third.
  • Left foot competence unusual for a right sided centre back, used for clearances, blocks and progressive passes.
  • Persistence with the game lost. Four won duels and a shot block in the last twenty minutes.

Weaknesses

  • Management of the space behind the line. Three negative actions including both goals he was directly involved in, plus two further incidents where the same error went unpunished.
  • Decision making on arrival when he steps out. He tackles rather than delays, and there is no cover behind him.
  • Awareness of his own line's shape. He scans often and does not always adjust his depth on what the scan shows.
  • One weak switch under pressure led directly to a turnover, though the sample there is too small to call it a pattern.

Where he fits

A back four defending a deeper line, or a back three taking the right sided position with cover inside him. In either setup the duelling is used and the space in behind is somebody else's problem as much as his. Playing him as the right centre back of a high line against a mobile front two, which is what this match asked of him, exposes his weakest competency repeatedly.

Ceiling

Depends on whether the line management is coachable. It is a decision making problem rather than an athletic one, and the fact that he already scans frequently suggests the habit is there and the interpretation is not. That is generally the more trainable version of the problem. He is 21, he has been through two Portuguese academies and there is time.

02  How this was built

Every judgement traces back to a timestamp.

Every action Käit was involved in was logged from broadcast footage and coded against the 360scouting centre back framework, four defensive competencies and two offensive. Each row carries the zone of origin, the zone the ball or the player finished in, whether he was under pressure, which part of the body made contact, whether a scan was visible before the action, and a written description in football action language. 55 actions in total, 29 in the first half and 26 in the second.

Scoring runs against defined competencies rather than a general impression, so the report can be argued with. If you think a rating is wrong, the actions behind it are listed and you can go and look.

Context, and why it changes the reading

Levadia scored inside four minutes. Paide levelled on 22 and the game stayed at 1-1 for half an hour. Levadia then scored three times in eighteen minutes, on 53, 65 and 71. Käit was directly involved in the first two of those three, and was on the pitch offering a build-up angle for the passage that led to the opening goal. Three Paide yellow cards fell between the 70th and 73rd minutes, around the third concession.

He was not defending a controlled game. He spent the first half in a team holding a high line against a front two, and the second half in a team losing shape. Roughly a third of his logged actions took place in the middle third rather than his own, which is a consequence of where his team asked him to defend rather than a choice he made.

Three limitations, stated plainly. This is one match and a cup tie rather than a league fixture, so selection and intensity may not represent either side's normal week. The footage is a single ball-following broadcast camera, so a share of his off-ball defending, line management and communication is simply not visible, and positional judgements are made only where the frame supports them. Actions were logged on a first viewing, so routine sideways distribution is under-represented relative to notable passes. The assessment is therefore weighted towards duels, recovery defending and on-ball actions, and a pass completion figure taken from this sample would flatter him.

04  Competency breakdown

Four defensive competencies, two offensive.

Defensive duelling

Clear strength

14 actions coded
12 positive, 1 neutral, 1 negative

Of those, 10 were duels proper
9 won, 1 lost

This is unambiguously his strongest competency and the reason the player is worth a report at all. He wins duels of every type in the sample. Aerially at 11:30 he anticipates the long ball from the opposition centre back and times his jump to the flight rather than to the runner, winning it cleanly. Side to side at 37:00 he anticipates the long ball, makes the recovery run, wins the physical contest and clears with his left foot. Frontally at 66:05 he steps out onto the carrier, opens his hips towards the touchline to shepherd him wide, waits for the heavy touch and explodes onto the ball.

Two actions stand out for what they say about recovery under pressure. At 81:25 he goes for an interception, fails to get it, corrects his body position rather than giving up on the action, delays the striker and then blocks the shot. At 75:03 he follows the striker's run, blocks a penetrating pass with his body, makes a recovery run back into the line, then communicates with his partner to switch positions and end the run safely. That is the only clear instance of visible organisation in the sample and it is worth noting precisely because the camera rarely shows it.

The one negative is the foul at 87:35. The neutral is a first half aerial duel from a throw in at 7:08 where the ball went over his head, though he still prevented the opponent heading it.

A caution on the number. Nine from ten in a single match is a strong signal, but duels won are more memorable than quiet losses, and this sample was logged from broadcast footage on a first viewing. Treat the direction as reliable and the exact rate as indicative.

Defending space in behind

Clear weakness

16 actions coded
5 positive, 8 neutral, 3 negative

This is the competency that decided the match for him. The mechanics of the individual recovery run are sound. He anticipates the long ball early and repeatedly starts his run before the pass is released rather than after it, visible at 0:50, 9:45 and 37:00. He scans over his shoulder while running, and in the strongest example of the match at 17:40 he covers the space behind, keeps the striker in front of him for the whole passage, then takes his first touch into space away from both goal and opponent rather than turning into trouble. At 42:58 and again at 45:20 in the second half he scans to locate his own line, decides to leave it, and blocks a shot on the edge of the box. Those are good decisions executed at speed.

The problem is not the run. It is the picture he acts on before the run starts. Both goals came from the same failure. At 52:25 he is left defending a 2v1, checks his shoulder, and chooses to cover the runner going towards the near post zone. That leaves the second attacker alone around the penalty spot and the finish is first time. The decision is defensible in isolation, but the outcome shows he backed the wrong option under a load he should not have been carrying alone.

At 64:22 the sequence is more damaging. The forward in front of him drops to receive, his partner steps out to follow, the ball is dropped and played first time over the line, and the space behind Käit is exploited. He scans, sees the space, and still does not adjust his depth before the pass is released. His recovery run does not reach the runner.

A third action at 61:34 shows the same pattern without the punishment. He covers his partner stepping out, then steps out himself to press without checking his shoulder first, and the space behind him is available. Levadia fail to use it. At 32:37 the line is out of shape with no scan to locate it. Four incidents of the same type across 90 minutes is a pattern rather than an accident.

The honest read is that his individual recovery defending is a strength and his line management is not. He reads the ball well and reads the space behind him poorly. Some of that is structural, since Paide defended high against a front two and his partner stepped out repeatedly, but the scans that would let him solve it are visible in the footage and he does not always act on what they show.

Leaving position to press

Clear weakness

7 actions coded
2 positive, 3 neutral, 2 negative

On the numbers this is his weakest competency, and it is closely related to the space in behind problem. The two positives show what it looks like when he gets it right. At 26:44 he steps out onto the midfielder, forces the back pass, then follows the ball towards the opposition box to press the centre back and delays the attack long enough for his own lines to recover. At 79:18 he follows the striker's run, steps out, stays in front of him and forces another back pass. In both cases he arrives goal side and stays there.

The two negatives are the same mistake twice. At 44:45 he steps out to cover the striker and leaves the channel between himself and the right back open. The opposition attack that space, his recovery run is late, and only an offside flag prevents a chance. At 88:05 he steps out to delay the striker, fails to make the tackle and is simply passed. At 87:35, coded under duelling, he steps out in the opposition half, mistimes a standing tackle and pulls the opponent for a foul. The 80:58 action falls in the same family, where he steps out, mistimes the tackle and has to recover at full speed.

The pattern is that his decision to step out is usually reasonable and his execution on arrival is not. He commits to the tackle rather than delaying, and when the tackle misses there is nobody behind him. Arriving side on and shepherding rather than lunging would convert several of these from negative to neutral.

At 44:05 he takes his position in the line, covers the space behind the full back, scans the box for runs, waits for the attacker to take a long touch and attacks the ball to clear it from his own box. That is a well executed defensive action.

One action across 90 minutes is not an assessment. Levadia's 4-1-3-2 attacked centrally through the front two and the game produced very few crosses into Paide's box. This competency needs a second match, ideally against a side that plays with width, before anything can be said about it.

Build-up against low block

Quiet strength

13 actions coded
6 positive, 7 neutral, 0 negative

More advanced than the raw split suggests. The repeatable behaviours are all present. He consistently offers a support angle, whether for the keeper, the full back or his centre back partner, and he receives with an open body rather than facing his own goal. A scan before receiving or before releasing is visible in the large majority of these actions. His first touch is regularly taken into space rather than to feet, which buys him the metre he needs to look up.

Three actions show genuine progressive quality. At 13:50 he intercepts a loose ball in his own half, carries it towards the middle of the pitch, scans, and finds a pass into the final third on the left which his teammate receives. At 45:47 he receives from the keeper, takes his touch into space, carries forward unopposed because the opposition do not step out, keeps carrying, scans again and plays a long pass to a teammate on the left of the final third, and the move ends with a shot off target. At 50:35 he receives from the full back in the middle third, scans, finds a teammate between the lines and plays the pass first time, cutting out two opponents.

There is also evidence of understanding beyond the pass itself. At 46:00 he plays to the full back and then drags the opposition striker away with him to open the return angle back to the keeper. That is a small action and it is the kind of thing that separates a centre back who executes from one who understands the structure he is in.

Two qualifications. He was almost never made uncomfortable in this phase, because Levadia's front two pressed the keeper and the wide players rather than pressing him directly, so most of these actions were taken with time. And the sample under-represents routine sideways distribution, so a completion figure taken from it would flatter him. What can be said with confidence is that when he had time he used it well, and that he made no unsuccessful pass in this category across 90 minutes.

Build-up under pressure

Sample too small

3 actions coded
1 positive, 0 neutral, 2 negative

Too small a sample to grade, and the three actions point in different directions. At 33:05 he offers the support angle for the keeper, takes his first touch into space, carries the ball out and finds the full back. That is the competency working. At 38:07 he creates the angle for the full back, takes the touch into space, carries, and then plays a weak switch across the field which is intercepted. At 3:30 he offers the support angle for the keeper, the pressing player covers the passing lane so the ball never reaches him, and Levadia score from the keeper's misplaced pass.

Involvement is not culpability. He is not at fault for the opening goal. He offered a legitimate angle and the lane was covered by an opponent. It is logged because he was part of the passage, not because the error was his. The same distinction matters in reverse for the 53rd and 65th minute goals, where the involvement was direct and the responsibility was his.

05  Foot use, scanning and game state

Three things the tally does not show.

Foot use

He is listed and plays as right footed, and 19 of his 30 ball contacts came off the right. But seven came off the left, and they were not all safety-first clearances. He cleared with his left at 37:00, blocked with his left at 45:20, and made progressive passes with his left at 27:28, 50:10 and 86:40. For a right sided centre back in a back four that level of left foot competence is a genuine asset, and it should be checked in a second viewing.

Scanning

Visible in 46 of 55 actions, consistent across both phases. The caveat established earlier applies in reverse here. Because he scans so often, the actions where he did not, at 32:37 and 61:34, read as decisions rather than habits. No conclusions are drawn on pace or aerial dominance in absolute terms: broadcast footage without a reference point does not support them, and there is no data available for the Estonian top flight to check them against.

The obvious reading is that he raised his level once the game turned, and that is worth stating because it runs against the impression that Paide collapsed after the second goal. The more careful reading is that the mix of actions changed. Nine of his ten duels came in the second half, and duels are the category he wins most, so some of the improvement is compositional rather than a change in performance.

What can be said without qualification is that he did not stop competing. That is a useful thing to know about a 21 year old in a team that collected three bookings in three minutes around the third concession.

08  Recommendation

Worth continued observation. Two or three matches, chosen deliberately.

At least one league fixture rather than a cup tie. At least one against a side that attacks with width, to give the defending crosses competency a sample. At least one where Paide defend a deeper line, to see whether the space in behind problem is his or his team's.

The question a second viewing has to answer

Whether the four incidents of poor depth management were caused by his own scanning, by his partner stepping out, or by the height of the line he was told to hold. This match does not separate those three and the answer changes the assessment materially.

The question that is not answerable from footage

Why a player who was on the books at Porto and Rio Ave is in the Estonian top flight at 21. Any recruiter reading this report will ask it, and it needs answering away from the pitch, alongside contract length, representation and whether Paide would sell.
